\slide{
  Until we invented Zerocracy, we struggled like you struggle now.
  Programmers made too many \emph{mistakes}, took too much \emph{time},
  and demanded to be paid for \emph{40-hour weeks} even when no progress was made.
  Worse, relying on a single programmer was always a \emph{risk}.}

\slide{
  Here is how Zerocracy works.
  You fund your project, programmers apply, and you let them contribute to your codebase.
  You pay a \emph{fixed fee} per pull request, only after you \emph{verify} and \emph{accept} it.
  You never pay for time, only for code that meets your \emph{quality bar}.}

\slide{
  This changes everything.
  Programmers deliver \emph{faster} because they earn more by completing more tasks.
  Quality stays high because rejected work means \emph{no payment}.
  Deadlines become \emph{predictable} because incentives are aligned.}

\slide{
  You no longer depend on a single programmer.
  Dozens of them collaborate, \emph{decomposing} large tasks into smaller ones.
  If one disappears, others continue.
  Your project moves forward \emph{regardless}.}
